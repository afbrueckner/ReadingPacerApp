Volcanoes are openings in the Earth's crust that allow molten rock, ash, and gases to escape onto the surface. When magma builds up pressure beneath a volcano, an eruption can occur. Some eruptions are explosive and send ash high into the atmosphere, while others produce gentle flows of liquid lava. Scientists monitor volcanic activity by measuring seismic waves, gas emissions, and ground deformation. Understanding these signs helps warning systems protect nearby communities from unexpected hazards.

Topic Preview: Ocean Hydrothermal Vents

Key Focus: Deep ocean ecosystems thrive without sunlight near superheated volcanic vents.

Guiding Question: How do living creatures survive on the ocean floor without sunlight?
Step 2: Read & Recite
Hydrothermal vents are fissures on the ocean floor that discharge geothermally heated water. Despite absolute darkness and extreme pressure, complex ecosystems thrive around them. Instead of sunlight, specialized bacteria rely on chemosynthesis, converting chemicals like hydrogen sulfide into energy. Giant tube worms and blind shrimp depend on these bacteria for survival.
